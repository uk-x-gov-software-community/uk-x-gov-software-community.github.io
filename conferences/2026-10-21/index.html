GovDev Midlands 2026

The venue

How to get there

The event is taking place in the Kerv Digital office at First Floor, City Buildings, Carrington Street, Nottingham, NG1 7FG.

  • Bus : We are opposite the Bus Station (Broadmarsh)
  • Car : Broadmarsh bus station has a car park, there’s also an NCP. The entrance to Broadmarsh is over the mini roundabout, SatNav tries to make you turn right.
    Queens Road NG2 3DW- reasonable in price and near to the office, also. If you can get on 3 or 4 it will take you to the level, to get in to the station via, over the tram line.
  • Train : We are on the same road as the Train station, just turn right if you come out of the main entrance.
  • Tram : Walk to the front of the train station (same place as above) and turn right
  • Walking : We’re right above Café Nero, it’s the door just to the left of Subway. There should be a security guard to let you in if you don’t have a pass.

On the day

Please remember to bring a valid form of ID and preferably a Civil Service pass. On the day you will also be given a conference pass to keep with you at all times, so that you can access the areas reserved for the event. Wifi will be available at the venue.

Food and beverages, including tea and coffee, will be provided, please contact the organising committee if you have any specific request (govdev-midlands@uk-x-gov-software-community.org.uk).

The venue also has cutlery, glasses and mugs for everyone to use: however, as we will use the office premises we recommend bringing your own water bottle and/or hot drinks cup to reduce waste.

Talk abstracts

Invited talks
Digital Driving License

What does it really take to turn a physical driving licence into a trusted digital credential? In this talk I’ll give a technical tour of the work we’ve been doing to digitise the driving licence, from the foundations that make a digital credential secure and trustworthy, through the journey a licence takes over its lifetime, to the real-world impact of putting identity in people’s pockets.

Speaker: Alex Clements, DVLA

Terraforming our Team: Escaping ClickOps, One Project at a Time

Infrastructure as Code sounds simple - stop clicking buttons and start writing code. The reality is a little messier. In this talk, I’ll share our Data Engineering team’s journey adopting Terraform, from building new projects with Infrastructure as Code to bringing existing infrastructure along for the ride. I’ll cover how our approach has evolved as we’ve learnt more, why we’ve had to revisit some of our earlier decisions, and the challenges of creating consistent practices across a team. Expect practical lessons, things we’d do differently, and an honest look at escaping ClickOps, one project at a time.

Speaker: Alissa Westley, DfT

From Idea to Impact: How AI is Solving Real Public Sector Challenges

Join Mike Chappell, Director of Technology @ Kerv as he shares practical insights from delivering digital and AI-driven solutions across the public and private sectors. Drawing on experience supporting government organisations, regulated industries and complex transformation programmes, Mike will move beyond the hype to explore how AI is being applied to solve real business and operational challenges.

This session will provide an honest perspective on what it takes to successfully design, build and deploy AI solutions at scale, sharing lessons learned, common pitfalls and examples of where organisations are already seeing measurable value. Expect practical insights, real-world stories and a look at how AI is helping organisations improve services, empower employees and deliver better outcomes for the people they serve.

Speaker: Mike Chappell, Kerv

Lightning talks
Confessions of an Introvert in Tech

As a developer, my ideal day involved working writing and avoiding unnecessary human interaction. I spent years believing that successful people were the loudest voices in the room. But as my career evolved, I discovered that influence isn’t about talking the most it’s about listening, learning and helping others succeed.

Speaker: Shababa Govani Khatau (East West Rail)

How to lose your female Engineers: Lessons from a working group

This talk is a brief look at the lessons we have learned from setting up a working group which aims to improve the gender diversity in the MoJ’s engineering roles. We will talk about some of the hurdles we have identified, what has gone well, and what comes next.

Speaker: Natalie Wood (MoJ)

Neuro-inclusive by design: what software engineering gets right

Exploring the working practices, culture and norms that make software engineering such a good fit for many neurodivergent people.

Speaker: Gilaine Young (Education)

Back to front: how data leaks through architectural cracks

A high level run through actions which led to a recent data breach. Business rules were moved from a back end server to a new client-side React app, resulting in unfiltered data being sent to users, who could view that data in their browser’s network request logs even though it wasn’t displayed on the page.

Speaker: Alex Eccles (HO)

You say waterfall and say agile, but we can’t call the whole thing off

We have been working with the Scottish Government to integrate their EPB register with the one for the rest of the UK. Between our two teams we have very different ways of approaching project management, work organisation and tracking. We have had to work together, adapting our approaches to accommodate each other. This talk will go through how we have been able to manage differences and pit falls so that in the end everything was fine!

Speaker: Christine Horrocks (MHCLG)

Building the front door to government tech

Office of the Chief Technology Officer (OCTO), a directorate in Government Digital Service, leads and supports several communities of practice for engineers, architects, and IT operations professionals across the public sector, but joining one often relies on word-of-mouth, and signposting to guidance isn’t always clear. This talk explores our research into a new service bringing together technical guidance, tools, and best practice alongside our communities of practice. Drawing on user research with community members about what they value and feel is missing, we will share what’s working well today, where the gaps are, and how a more joined-up approach could make it easier for practitioners to find guidance, connect with peers, and grow professionally.

Speaker: Lianne Williams and Sarah Handyside, DSIT
